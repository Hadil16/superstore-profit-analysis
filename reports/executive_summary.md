# Executive summary: Superstore profitability, 2014-2017

**Prepared for:** Retail management  **Scope:** 9,994 order lines, $2.3M in sales, $286K in profit (12.5% margin)

## Bottom line

The company is profitable overall, but **discounts above 20% destroy profit**. They are rare (14% of order lines) yet they lost $135K, about **one third** of the profit earned on all other lines.

## What we found

1. **Discounts above 20% lose money.** The margin is +29.5% with no discount, -15.3% between 21% and 40%, and -77.4% above 40%.
2. **Furniture is the weak category.** It sells as much as Office Supplies but keeps only 2.5% as margin (17% for the other categories).
3. **Central is the weak region** (7.9% margin) and gives the largest discounts (24% on average, against 10.9% in the West).
4. **Three sub-categories lose money:** Tables (-$17.7K), Bookcases (-$3.5K) and Supplies (-$1.2K).
5. **Customer segments are similar** (11.5% to 14.0% margin), so segments are not the problem.

## What we recommend

| Priority | Action | How we will know it works |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cap discounts at 20%, with manager approval above | Share of lines above 20% discount, overall margin |
| 2 | Review Tables pricing and discount policy | Tables margin moves above 0% |
| 3 | Investigate Supplies costs and prices (its losses are not discount-driven) | Supplies margin |
| 4 | Align Central discount rules with the West | Central margin and average discount |

## Caveats

The link between discounts and losses is strong and logical, but it is an association, not a proven cause. This is a public sample dataset, so the figures illustrate a method. Profit is taken as given, with no cost detail.
